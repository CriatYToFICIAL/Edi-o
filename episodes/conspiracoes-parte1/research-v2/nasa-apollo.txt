Schmitt Apollo 17, Lunar Module Pilot Apollo’s Goals The national effort that enabled Astronaut Neil Armstrong to speak those words as he stepped onto the lunar surface fulfilled a dream as old as humanity. Project Apollo’s goals went beyond landing Americans on the moon and returning them safely to Earth. They included: Establishing the technology to meet other national interests in space. Achieving preeminence in space for the United States. Carrying out a program of scientific exploration of the Moon. Developing human capability to work in the lunar environment. Rocket and Spacecraft The flight mode, lunar orbit rendezvous , was selected in 1962. The boosters for the program were the Saturn IB for Earth orbit flights and the Saturn V for lunar flights. Apollo was a three-part spacecraft: the command module (CM), the crew’s quarters and flight control section; the service module (SM) for the propulsion and spacecraft support systems (when together, the two modules are called CSM); and the lunar module (LM), to take two of the crew to the lunar surface, support them on the Moon, and return them to the CSM in lunar orbit. The Apollo Missions Apollo 1 On Jan. 27, 1967, tragedy struck on the launch pad at Cape Kennedy during a preflight test for Apollo 204. About Apollo 1 Apollo 4 The uncrewed Apollo 4 mission was the first all-up test of the three-stage Saturn V rocket. About Apollo 4 Apollo 5 The primary goal of the uncrewed Apollo 5 mission was to complete the first test flight of the Lunar Module. About Apollo 5 Apollo 6 The uncrewed Apollo 6 mission was the final qualification of the Saturn V launch vehicle and Apollo spacecraft for crewed Apollo missions. About Apollo 6 Apollo 7 The first crewed mission of the Apollo program lifted off on October 11, 1968, for a 10-day flight in Earth orbit. About Apollo 7 Apollo 8 Frank Borman, James Lovell, and William Anders made the first crewed mission to the vicinity of the Moon in December 1968. About Apollo 8 Apollo 9 James McDivitt, Russell Schweickart, and David Scott make the first flight of the full Apollo spacecraft in March 1969. About Apollo 9 Apollo 10 Astronauts Thomas Stafford, John Young, and Eugene Cernan test all the components for a lunar landing mission, except landing on the Moon, in May 1969. About Apollo 10 Apollo 11 Neil Armstrong, Edwin"Buzz" Aldrin, and Michael Collins make history as Armstrong and Aldrin become the first humans to walk on the Moon in July 1969. About Apollo 11 Apollo 12 The second lunar landing of the Apollo Program was completed by Charles "Pete" Conrad, Richard Gordon, and Alan Bean in November 1969. About Apollo 12 Apollo 13 An explosion on board forced Apollo 13 to circle the Moon without landing. Through the valiant efforts of the crew and ground team, the astronauts safely returned to Earth. About Apollo 13 Apollo 14 In January 1971, astronauts walked on the Moon for the third time, this time with Alan Shepard and Edgar Mitchell visiting the Moon's Fra Mauro region. About Apollo 14 Apollo 15 David Scott, Alfred Worden, and James Irwin launched to the Moon for the fourth Apollo lunar landing in July 1971. It was the first time the Lunar Roving Vehicle was driven on the Moon. About Apollo 15 Apollo 16 In April 1972, John Young, Charles Duke and Ken Mattingly made the penultimate lunar landing mission of the Apollo Program, visiting the Moon's Descartes Highlands. About Apollo 16 Apollo 17 The final Apollo mission to the Moon took place in December 1972. Astronauts Eugene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt collected a record amount of lunar samples over three moonwalks. About Apollo 17 American Flags on the Moon Flying High Apollo astronauts planted six U.S. flags on the surface of the Moon from 1969 to 1972. This Apollo 11 flag-raising was strictly a symbolic activity, as the United Nations Treaty on Outer Space precluded any territorial claim. Nevertheless, there were domestic and international debates over the appropriateness of the event. Furthermore, there were technical challenges that needed to be solved to erect the American flag. Read “Where No Flag Has Flown Before” about Flying High Astronaut Edwin E. “Buzz” Aldrin Jr., lunar module pilot of the first lunar landing mission, poses for a photo beside the deployed United States flag during an Apollo 11 extravehicular activity (EVA) on the lunar surface. Apollo Lunar Surface Journal The Apollo Lunar Surface Journal is a record of the lunar surface operations conducted by the six pairs of astronauts who landed on the Moon from 1969 through 1972.
